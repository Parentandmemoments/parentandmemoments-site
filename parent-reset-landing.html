You love your child. That part is not in question. But somewhere between the school runs and the work calls and the bedtime battles, you started losing yourself — and losing them — in a way nobody warned you about.

You're not failing. But you're not okay either. And the worst part? You can feel the distance opening up between you and your child — that gap where connection used to be — and you don't quite know how to close it.

This is what parenting burnout looks like from the inside. Not dramatic. Not a crisis anyone can see. Just a quiet, creeping depletion that makes you less present, less patient, and less like yourself — one week at a time.

Does any of this sound familiar?

You didn't plan to end up here. But here you are.

  • You lie awake replaying the moments you missed, the things you said, the ways you fell short — even when you tried your best.

  • Sundays fill you with a low dread you can't quite explain — not because you don't love your family, but because you're not sure how much longer you can keep this up.
